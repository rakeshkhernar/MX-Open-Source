Help & Guide

What is MX?

MX (Manipulation Index) is a browser extension that surfaces sentiment, tone, and manipulation signals in the text you read. It runs entirely in your browser — no data ever leaves your device.

MX combines three layers of analysis into a single at-a-glance gauge: word-level sentiment (positive/negative vocabulary), tone signals (emotional patterns like hostility, alarm, warmth, optimism), and manipulation detection (fear framing, urgency pressure, divisive rhetoric, and structural persuasion patterns like slippery slopes, false dichotomies, and strawman framing). Over 2,700 pattern detectors in total.

Use it as a well-informed second opinion — it flags patterns, not truth.

How It Works
👁️

Automatic Analysis

MX samples visible text and scores sentiment, tone signals, and manipulation patterns in real-time as you browse. Headlines count 5× more than body text. Quoted text is discounted 80%. Shopping sites, recipes, and sports scores are recognized to avoid false alarms.

🎭

Tone Signals

Detects emotional tone patterns — both negative (hostility, alarm, distress, contempt) and positive (admiration, warmth, optimism, celebration). Tone signals can override word-level sentiment when they strongly disagree.

📊

Manipulation Detection

Flags urgency pressure, fear framing, divisive rhetoric, and emotional push language. If multiple patterns cross a threshold, the content is flagged as manipulative.

🎨

On-Page Gauge

A small gauge indicator appears in the corner of every page — green for positive, yellow for neutral, red for negative. The needle position shows sentiment strength. Click it for a quick summary, then hit the chart icon for the full MX Report breakdown. Drag it anywhere you like — your position is saved across tabs.

🍩

Dual-Ring Donut Chart

The MX Report shows a dual-ring visualization: the outer ring displays positive signals (admiration, warmth, optimism, celebration) and the inner ring shows negative and manipulation signals. Ring fill is proportional to signal count.

🔍

Full Transparency

Click any category chip or donut segment in the MX Report to see the exact words and phrases that were matched. Every rating has visible datapoints behind it — no black box.

⚙️

Customizable

Adjust size, opacity, position, and site exclusions. Set manipulation sensitivity to low (fewer alerts), medium (balanced), or high (catches borderline cases). Right-click the extension icon and choose "Options" to open settings.

Understanding the Indicators

MX uses a gauge meter on every page to show the overall tone. The needle swings from green (positive) through yellow (neutral) to red (negative). A red warning badge appears when manipulation patterns are detected. Here's what each level means:

Positive

Content with uplifting, encouraging, or optimistic tone. Confirmed by positive tone signals like admiration, warmth, and celebration.

Neutral Tone

No manipulation techniques detected. Minimal emotional signals — but neutral tone doesn't guarantee accuracy.

Negative

Content with hostile, alarming, or distressing tone. Tone signals such as hostility, alarm, and distress reinforce this classification.

Manipulative

Strong manipulation patterns detected — fear framing, urgency pressure, or divisive rhetoric. Read critically and check other sources.

Tone Signals

Tone signals bridge the gap between individual word sentiment and full manipulation patterns. They detect emotional overtones in the content:

Negative Tone Signals

Hostility
Violence, aggression, war, conflict, anger — language of confrontation and harm.
Alarm
Crisis, danger, threat, collapse, devastation — language that creates a sense of emergency.
Distress
Death, suffering, grief, loss, trauma — language of pain and despair.
Contempt
Disgust, corruption, scandal, failure, betrayal — language of moral judgement and disdain.

Positive Tone Signals

Admiration
Praise, excellence, triumph, inspiration — language of respect and awe.
Warmth
Love, kindness, compassion, gratitude — language of care and connection.
Optimism
Hope, progress, improvement, recovery — language of possibility and growth.
Celebration
Joy, happiness, delight, humor — language of festivity and shared excitement.

Manipulation Pattern Types

Unlike tone signals (which detect emotional language that may be perfectly legitimate), manipulation patterns flag language specifically designed to bypass critical thinking. MX uses both vocabulary matching and structural regex patterns to detect these — including slippery slope arguments, false dichotomies, strawman framing, whataboutism, gaslighting patterns, emotional blackmail, and bandwagon pressure:

Fear-based
Alarmist framing designed to trigger anxiety, panic, or dread in the reader.
Divisive
"Us vs. them" framing, group vilification, or dehumanizing language.
Urgency
Time pressure designed to short-circuit deliberation and provoke impulsive action.
Emotional
Language that pushes outrage or indignation to bypass reasoning and override judgement.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is my browsing data collected?

No. All analysis runs locally in your browser. No text, scores, or browsing data is ever sent to external servers.

What does "manipulative" mean?

It means multiple manipulation patterns (fear framing, urgency pressure, divisive rhetoric) were detected above a proportional threshold. It does not imply the content is factually false.

Why does a news site show negative when the writing seems professional?

Tone signals detect the emotional content of what's being reported, not the writing quality. A well-written article about war, violence, and crisis will correctly show negative tone — because the subject matter is negative.

What are tone signals?

Tone signals are emotional patterns detected in the text — eight categories covering both negative (hostility, alarm, distress, contempt) and positive (admiration, warmth, optimism, celebration) tones. When they strongly disagree with word-level sentiment, they correct the overall score.

How accurate is MX?

MX has been validated against 100+ real websites across news, social media, tech, sports, shopping, and entertainment categories. It reliably catches manipulation patterns, tone shifts, and sentiment direction. Like any analysis tool, it works best as a second opinion alongside your own judgement.

Can I see what words triggered each score?

Yes! Click any chip or donut segment in the MX Report breakdown to expand the matched words. Full transparency — no black box.

Why does some content show "low confidence"?

Confidence depends on how much text was analyzed and how many signals were found. Pages with very little text (image-heavy sites, login screens) naturally have less data to work with.

Can I disable MX on certain sites?

Yes. Click the gauge and use the tooltip to disable on the current site, or go to Settings → Disabled Sites and add domains manually.

Tips for Critical Reading

Verify claims: Look for primary sources and independent corroboration.

Check context: Dates, scope, and framing often change meaning.

Compare coverage: Open the same story on multiple sites and compare MX scores.

Notice strong reactions: If something triggers a strong emotional response, take a moment to re-read before sharing or reacting.

Commercial ≠ manipulative: Shopping sites using "limited time offer" is expected marketing, not manipulation.
